# `_reference/` — apparatus *about* the corpus

Material that describes, teaches or surveys the Vedic corpus without being part of it. These are
**not** śākhā-bound and must never be filed under a śākhā: a work about svara notation belongs to
no branch, and putting it under one would assert a provenance it does not have.

Nothing here is a source text. Nothing here is `sourced` evidence for any node.

## ⭐ Why the kamakoti link matters beyond this one file

`kamakoti.org` is the **first canonical maṭha source cited anywhere in this repo**, and there is a
standing open item pointing at exactly that domain.

The Institutional Register's framing assets rest on the Kanchi *Veda Samrakṣaṇam* scheme — which
leads with the Vyākaraṇa Mahābhāṣya, *"knowledge of the Vedas is complete only when the meaning is
learnt,"* and organises its programme by the classical stages **adhīti → bodha → …**, with *bodha*
separately institutionalised. That is the argument that the artha layer is **a stage of the
tradition's own stated programme, not a foreign import** — and it is the sentence an approach to
the maṭha would be built on.

It is currently carried as `[verify full scheme from kamakoti.org]`. **This is that site.** Pulling
the scheme to source from here would close the open item and put a primary citation under the
project's strongest institutional argument.

> ### ⚠ The distinction that governs this section
>
> **Rule 9 says a typeset public-domain text is public domain** — setting the Taittirīya Brāhmaṇa
> in LaTeX adds nothing copyrightable, so the notice on it does not bind.
>
> **A modern translation is the opposite case.** The Sanskrit is public domain; the translator's
> English is **original expression and is theirs**. Griffith and Wilson are ours because their
> copyright expired, not because the underlying text is old.
>
> **Being on Archive.org is not a licence.** Archive.org hosts in-copyright material routinely.
> Check the work's own date and rights, never the host's.

## Rule

Anything filed here must be **about** the corpus. If a work contains mūla text, it belongs under
its śākhā — `_reference/` is not a convenience drawer for things that are awkward to place.
